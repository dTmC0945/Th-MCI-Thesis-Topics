1.2 Analog Computer for Solving ODEs ¶

Analog computer are a class of devices whereby continuously variable physical quantities, (i.e., electrical potential) are represented in a way that is analougous to the corresponding quantities in the problem to be solved. The analog system is set up according to initial conditions and then allowed to change freely.

Answers to the problem are obtained by measuring the variables in the analog model and are seeing a resurgence as a good alternative to digital computers.

There are numerous examples such as the Phillip’s Machine which was used to determine the economic process at a national level using fluidic logic, or the Robert’s Machine which was used to determine the tides.

An interesting article by Quanta magazine on Analog computers can be seen here.

The work of this thesis is to design, test, and construct an electrical circuit which is capable of solving 1st- and 2nd-order ODEs given to the circuit as input.

As an example, using the circuit, the behaviour of a mass-damper-spring system will be simulated: 

\[m \frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + c\frac{dx}{dt} + kx = f(x),
\]

where the values of the mass (\(m\)), damper (\(c\)) and spring (\(k\)) will be set using a signal generator.

To achive the aforementioned effectively, the following are required:

  • Working understanding with PCBs,
  • Circuit design and soldering.
  • Understanding ODEs are a plus.
