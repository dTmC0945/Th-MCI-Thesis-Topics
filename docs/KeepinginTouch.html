4.1 Keeping in Touch

Let’s discuss the overall methods which we can communicate our work together to produce a respectable engineering thesis.

4.1.1 Weekly Reports

It is a good idea that I receive weekly reports written in English from you.

  • It keeps you on track towards your degree, whether it is B.Sc and M.Sc by making you read and think more about the thesis.

  • It allows to measure your progress in a qualitative way which will be a significant part of your thesis grade.

I will make comments on your reports. (...) such as grammar, logic, contents, and the overall logic of your work etc. By the time you start to write your great theses, which I am sure it will be great, your command of English will be such that writing in it no longer terrifies you.

I also got to enjoy benefit of amortizing the time of revising your later works.

A weekly report is expected from

  • All B.Sc Students starting from submitting their BA1 form,

  • All M.Sc Students starting from submitting their MA1 form.

A weekly report can be one of the following:

  • A rough draft of your thesis.

  • A summary of the academic paper(s) you are reading.

    • Now, if you read only 1-2 pages, summarize those 1-2 pages.

  • A summary of anything that you read which is relevant to the thesis work.

4.1.2 Weekly Report Template Structure

The list given below is just a guideline, and you are encouraged to adapt it to suit the specific requirements and goals of your research project.

Project Progress

  • Briefly summarize the progress made on your research project during the past week.

  • Highlight any significant achievements, milestones, or challenges encountered.

Literature Review

  • Provide an overview of the relevant literature you have reviewed or studied.

  • Summarize key findings, methodologies, and concepts related to your research.

Methodology and Experimentation

  • Describe the methodologies, techniques, or experiments you have employed or conducted.

  • Include details such as materials, procedures, data collection methods, and any modifications made.

Results and Analysis

  • Present and discuss the results obtained from your experiments or data analysis.

  • Include any tables, graphs, or figures to support your findings. Provide a critical analysis/interpretation of the results.

Challenges and Solutions

  • Identify any challenges or obstacles you encountered during your research.

  • Discuss the strategies or solutions you implemented to overcome these challenges.

Future Work

  • Outline your planned next steps and objectives for the upcoming week.

  • Specify any additional experiments, analyses, or literature review tasks you intend to undertake.

Questions and Discussion Points

  • List any questions or concerns you have for further discussion during the group meeting.

  • Include any topics you would like to discuss with your supervisor or fellow researchers.

Personal Development and Learning

  • Reflect on any new skills, knowledge, or insights gained during the week.

  • Discuss any seminars, workshops, or training sessions attended.

Other Updates

  • Mention any relevant updates, announcements, or collaborations with other researchers or teams.

4.1.4 GitHub Repo

As mentioned within the commends of the .md file, it is mandatory for all students to create a repo on Github/CodeBerg/Gitlab for their weekly reports and all relevant code and additional documentation in there.

The repo can be private in the duration of the work. Please submit your weekly report into this repo every week by updating the singular README.md file. If the repo is private you have to add the supervisor as a collaborator for access.

The repo shared must be named in the following manner: 



MCI-Th-<DEGREE>-<THESIS-NAME>

Where <DEGREE> is the degree currently being pursued (i.e., BSc or MSc) and <THESIS-NAME> is the name of the thesis where each space is replaced with dash.

For example: Jason Brigham, who is a B.Sc students working on "Effect of Slit Depth in Skewed Rotor Geometries" would open a repo under the name: 



MCI-Th-BSc-Effect-of-Slit-Depth-in-Skewed-Rotor-Geometries

He would keep his research private but would give his supervisor access to this repo while also keeping a repo to himself where he prototypes ideas and only putting relatively polised work into the shared repo.

A Template repo has been provided which you can use to structure your data and ideas, which can be accessed here.

Information: Personal Repos

I am well aware that some would have prior experience with GitHub and may have developed their own way of doing things. In that case, you can keep a repo which is completely your own, as mentioned in the previous example. This could be prototype ideas, pre-configured files or uncommented code.

Once the data/files have been sorted you can then push item you want the supervisor to see to the joint repo.

4.1.5 Meeting Structure

By default, unless a prior agreement has been reached, it is assumed a bi-weekly meeting is to be taken place. However, if requested with adequate reasoning, the meeting structure could be changed. The meetings can be face-to-face or through online. In the event a student requires an additional meeting between two predetermined meetings, it will be arranged.

If you cannot attend the meeting, kindly notify me via email and briefly explain the reason for your absence.
