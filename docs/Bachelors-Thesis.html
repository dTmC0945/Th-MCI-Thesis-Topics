3.1 Development of a GUI and Simulation for Induction Motors

Induction machines are the working horse of industry as majority of electrical machines from a simple fan on your desk to list and cranes use induction machines. Therefore the study and the analysis of these machines would prove invaluable. This project will involve the dynamic modeling of a 3-phase induction machine using Python or C/C++. This work will then be designed in a way which is user accessible, whereby a GUI will be implemented with variables to allow the end-user to tweak the parameters and perform the simulations to see the effects.

To achieve the required modeling, the students should have a working knowledge of:

  • A good working knowledge of C/C++ or Python,
  • A working understanding of induction machines.
  • An interest in learning how to design a GUI for the written program

The outcome will be a fully functional software platform which is changeable by the end-user.

3.2 Design and Implementation of Cyclic Redundancy Check for Transmission

A cyclic redundancy check is an error-detecting code used in digital communication networks and storage systems to detect accidental changes to raw data. Blocks of data that are transmitted get a short check value attached to the block code, based on the remainder of a polynomial division of their contents. When retrieved, the calculation is redone and, in the event the check values do not match (i.e., when an error occurs during transmission), an action can be taken against data corruption.

This project will involve an experimental design of a communication system involving two Arduinos. These two Arduinos will communicate with each other and check each transmitted message using CRCs. The user will introduce errors externally (i.e., drawing a magnet next to wires, or creating cross-talk) and observe the output from a display attached to the Arduinos.

To achieve this test bed, the students is required to have:

  • A good experience in programming C/C++,
  • Designing of logic circuits,
  • A good understanding of communication network topics.

The outcome will be a fully functional experimental test-bed which will be used as a showcase of communication concepts.

3.3 Design of a DC Motor Test Bench

The students should have knowledge of:

  • Circuit design,
  • CAD software experience,
  • Basic knowledge on electric machines is a plus.

DC Drives are one of the more versatile machines still used in industry due to their cost effective design and simple control methodologies. One of the advantages is their ability to adapt based on how their wiring is done for field and armature. This work will require the construction of a DC motor test bench for use in electric machine lectures.

The outcome will be a working platform where the machine dynamic can be changed based on how the connections are done. These connections will be designed in a way as to easily be changed by the user. The student shall also gain practical experience with electric drive design and work with manufacturing of a product which takes different parts of electrical engineering and combine them. The student will also contribute to the development of new hardware to be used by the next generation of students.

3.4 Simulation of GNSS within the Gazebo Environment

The problem he tackled was the fact that GNSS receivers have only very basic support in simulators for mobile robotics. The currently available simulated sensors have a very unfaithful error model of the estimated positions (usually only additive Gaussian noise or a small random walk). If you’ve ever worked with a real GNSS receiver, you know that this is far from what you get in reality.

One of the most important factors that influence the computed position and its covariance is the visibility of the satellites. It influences both the DOP (dilution of precision), i.e., the geometry of the visible parts of constellations, and the convergence of the position estimation algorithm.

Václav has implemented a raytracing algorithm that detects which satellites are hidden behind scene objects (buildings, vegetation, etc.) and computes a full-fledged RINEX observation log with only the unblocked signals or with some signals damaged (if they, e.g., passed through a tree). The algorithm can even simulate cycle slips for phase measurements if some satellite disappeared for a while and then was found again. You can use your favorite RINEX processing tool to get the position and covariance estimates.

Our future direction is making the simulation model more complete (including atmospheric effects), also implementing the position estimation part in Gazebo/ROS using the standard interfaces, and making the plugin actually usable by the community (as the current version is not yet production quality software).
